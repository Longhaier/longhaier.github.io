I don't know who wrote the original or whether the story is true. I found it worth sharing.

In the winter of 1976, I was nineteen. I worked in NASA's space shuttle lab in Houston and studied computer science at the nearby University of Houston. School, work, and sleep took up nearly every hour. Whenever I had a minute left over, I poured it into making music.

Lyrics were my weak spot, so I looked for a collaborator who could write songs and words. I met a friend named Valerie Johnson. I haven't heard from her since I left Texas more than twenty years ago, but she gave me the greatest encouragement at the start of my career. At nineteen, Valerie had already won more Texas poetry awards than I could count. I loved her writing. We wrote a number of pieces together, and I still think they had real character and originality.

One Saturday, Valerie invited me to a barbecue at her family's ranch. Her family had made a fortune in Texas oil and owned a huge ranch. They were very wealthy, and I admired Valerie even more for the way she dressed, the car she drove, and the modesty and sincerity with which she treated people. She knew how serious I was about music. The American record industry seemed far away and unfamiliar, and neither of us had a way into it.

There we were in the Texas countryside, with no idea what to do next.

“Visualize what you'll be doing in five years,” she said out of nowhere.

I paused.

She turned and pointed at me. “Tell me what you'd *most like* to be doing five years from now. What does your life look like then?” Before I could answer, she added, “Take your time. Think it through and tell me once you're sure.”

After a few minutes, I said, “First, I want to have a record out. I want people to like it and take it seriously. Second, I want to live somewhere with music everywhere, where I can work with world-class musicians every day.”

“Are you sure?”

I answered slowly and firmly, drawing it out. “Yesssssss!”

## Working backward

“Good,” Valerie said. “Let's work backward. If your record is out in year five, by year four you'll need to have signed a record deal.

“By year three you'll need a complete recording to play for a lot of record companies, right?

“By year two you'll need to be recording really good material.

“By the end of year one, all the material you want to record will need to be arranged, rehearsed, and ready.

“By month six, you'll need to have polished the unfinished pieces so you can review them one by one.

“By the end of month one, you'll need to finish the songs you're working on now.

“In week one, you'll need to make a list of everything that needs revision and everything that needs finishing.”

She smiled. “So now you know what you're doing next Monday, don't you?”

“Oh, and you also said you wanted to live somewhere full of music and work with great musicians,” she added. “If you're working with them in year five, by year four you'll probably need a workspace or recording studio of your own. In year three, you might start working with people in that scene. By year two, you should already be living in New York or Los Angeles.”

## The next step

The next year, in 1977, I left the NASA job that so many people envied, moved out of Houston, and went to Los Angeles.

It wasn't exactly five years, but the timing was close. In 1983, roughly six years later, my records started selling in Asia. I was spending nearly every waking hour working with some of the best musicians, from sunrise to sunset.

Whenever I feel most lost, I sit quietly and ask myself what I'd most like to see myself doing five years from now.

If you don't know your own answer, how can you ask another person or God to choose a direction or open a door for you? Remember that God has already put the power to choose in our hands.

If you often find yourself asking why your life turned out this way or that way, try asking yourself whether you know, very clearly, what you want.

If you don't know what you want, how can the Lord who loves you arrange it for you?

People around you can cheer you on as enthusiastically as they like, and the Lord who loves you can offer you comfort. But if you have yet to tell him clearly what you want, how can you blame God for failing to open a door?
