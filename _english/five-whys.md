## The Five Whys

### What is the Five Whys method?

When a problem arises, ask why it happened. Ask why again for each answer until you reach an underlying cause you can address. The method uses about five rounds of questioning and was developed and popularized at Toyota in Japan.

![Five Whys illustration](https://wiki.mbalib.com/w/images/8/8f/5why.jpg)

### Why learn it?

The first things we see are usually surface symptoms. An engineer new to this method might attribute the problem to a tight schedule, limited investment, understaffing, or aging equipment. Those explanations can be valid and difficult to change. Keep tracing the chain of events to find the particular step that allowed the problem to occur.

One principle to remember during Five Whys training is that process design deserves the team's attention when people make mistakes.

### How do you use it?

- Organize possible causes with a fishbone diagram.
- Record your findings in an analysis table.

![Example of a Five Whys analysis table](https://4.bp.blogspot.com/_ilVgx1VRxIY/SlLnm61ApQI/AAAAAAAAAKI/e81NW3lyYRc/s400/5-why4.png)

Follow more than one branch when the evidence points to multiple causes. Once you identify a cause, pair it with an action that addresses it.

For a productive session:

- Bring the right people together. For a difficult problem, consider a facilitator.
- Use paper or a whiteboard so everyone can see the analysis taking shape.
- Write down the problem and make sure everyone understands it.
- Separate distinct problems so you can analyze each one.
- Pay attention to cause and effect.
- Describe events as precisely as possible.
- Work one step at a time and avoid jumping to conclusions.
- Base the analysis on facts and what the team knows.
- Focus the discussion on the process.
- Look beyond explanations such as “human error” or “employee carelessness.”
- Create an honest, trusting environment.
- Keep going until you identify a cause that, if addressed, could prevent this type of incident from recurring.
- Consider the problem from the customer's point of view.
